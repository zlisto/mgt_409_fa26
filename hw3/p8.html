Homework 3: Campus Customs Product Search

Scenario and data

Scenario

Campus Customs is the Yale merch shop on Broadway. Associates often get questions like “do you have that Yale shirt with Handsome Dan?” or a customer holding up a phone video of a tee and asking for a size or color. The shop does not have a Uniqlo-style visual search stack—you will build a lightweight version for class.

Your job is to turn the inventory photos into a searchable text catalog, support typed text search (ranked matches + a static HTML page), and support video search: transcribe the ask, pick a frame, crop/zoom, describe, text-filter the catalog, then vision-compare against the top matches. Everything important lands in JSON first; HTML is for humans to review. Use the OpenAI API (vision- and audio-capable models as needed). Work one problem at a time with your vibe coder. Screenshot the problem you are on; do not paste this whole page. Zip your work as hw3.zip.

This homework uses Campus Customs / Yale Bulldog Blue as a local setting only. It is not a partnership. Stock quantities in the pack are synthetic for teaching.

Before you start, make a folder with prompts/ and output/. Add one script per problem as you go. The zip layout is the last problem.

Data

Download hw3_campus_customs.zip. Unzip it next to your project (or copy inventory.json and products/ into your working folder). The pack has a curated set of 20 product images and prices from the public Yale Bulldog Blue t-shirts & tops collection (small enough to index with vision in one sitting). Put the customer video/stills under media/ when your instructor posts them (see the pack README).

Problem 8: Judgment calls (8 points)

Agent note P8: judgment_calls.json — most/least trusted lookup; can discuss text vs video or no-match.

Step back from the model outputs. Which lookup result do you trust most, and which do you trust least—and why?

No Python script is required. Create output/judgment_calls.json yourself as a JSON object with two entries: most trusted and least trusted.

Each entry must include:

  • label: most_trusted or least_trusted
  • which: short id of the run (e.g. text query slug, video match, or no-match still)
  • rationale: plain English

Your two picks must refer to different runs. You may disagree with the model’s score or status if you explain why.
