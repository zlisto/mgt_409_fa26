Why AI Costs What It Costs

  • Tokenomics — the economics behind AI token prices

GPUs - The Chips That Power AI

  • GPU = graphics processing unit
  • Computer chips with thousands of small cores that do the same math in parallel
  • Main uses:
    • Computer graphics (i.e. gaming)
    • Blockchain (i.e. mining crypto)
    • AI (i.e. training and token generation)
  • Fast matrix multiplication — the main math operation in AI models

CPU vs GPU: Architecture

  • CPU (central processing unit) — a few powerful cores; great at complex, branching, one-at-a-time logic
  • GPU (graphics processing unit) — thousands of tiny cores; great at parallel simple computations
  • AI model computations are built for thousands of tiny processors

Scaling Up GPUs

  • Newer GPUs pack more transistors into the chip
    • More transistors = more computational power
  • AI accelerator - chips built to speed up AI computation
    • GPUs are the most common accelerator
    • Other AI accelerators - TPUs (Google), Trainium (Amazon)

GPU Supply Chain

  • NVIDIA designs most frontier AI GPUs — roughly ~80–90% of AI accelerator revenue
  • TSMC (Taiwan Seminconductor Manufacturing Company) fabricates nearly all of those AI GPUs
  • The GPU supply chain is very concentrated — a few firms design the chips, and one foundry builds most of them

How Much Power Does A GPU Use?

  • Chip only — H100 up to ~700 W; B200 ~1,000 W (NVIDIA)
  • Chip + rack share — often ~1.3–1.7 kW per accelerator (SemiAnalysis)
  • What’s in “rack power”?
    • Power supplies (conversion losses)
    • CPUs, fans, networking cards in the server
    • Facility cooling share (chillers / liquid loops — outside the box)
    • Switches / cables that connect the GPUs
  • AI datacenters are buildings full of racks of GPU servers
    • Photo: one server — 8× NVIDIA H200 — ≈ $361k (Broadberry)

One GPU ≈ One Household

  • Avg U.S. household electricity consumption ≈ 1.2 kW (EIA)
  • Modern AI GPU power consumption ≈ 1 kW (NVIDIA H100)
  • 1 GPU ≈ 1 household
  • Datacenters have thousands of GPUs

AI Datacenter Scale

  • Hyperscaler — a company that runs huge datacenters worldwide and sells cloud compute: Amazon (AWS), Microsoft (Azure), Google (GCP)
  • Meta — Llama 4 trained on >100,000 H100 GPUs (earnings)
  • SpaceX Colossus (Memphis, TN) — 200,000 H100 GPUs (xAI)
  • 100,000 H100 GPUs ≈ 150 MW
    • Big datacenters pay $0.05–$0.10 per kWh (homes often $0.25–$0.35)
    • 150 MW costs a datacenter ~$124M/year (SemiAnalysis)
  • OpenAI Stargate (Abilene, TX) — campus planned for ~1.2 GW (DCD)
  • 100 MW ≈ 100,000 homes of electricity (IEA)
    • Population of Abilene, TX ≈ 131,588 (~131 MW of electrical power)

Water For Cooling

  • GPUs get hot, so datacenters use water to cool them
  • 100 MW data center uses about 500,000 gallons/day (~2 million liters/day) (Bloomberg / IEA)
  • U.S. home uses about 300 gallons/day (EPA)
  • 100 MW datacenter water use is equivalent to ~1,500–2,000 homes
  • Google The Dalles (Oregon data-center campus) — in 2024 used ~1.2M gal/day (reporting)
    • Equivalent to 4,000 homes water use

Training AI With GPUs

  • Example: Llama 3 405B was trained with 16,384 H100s running for 54 days (Meta)
  • Chip cost: 16k chips × $30k per chip ≈ $480M
  • Power usage (chip + rack ~1.5 kW) — 16k × 1.5 × 24 × 54 ≈ 32M kWh
  • Power cost — 32M kWh × $0.10/kWh ≈ $3M
  • Chips dominate; electricity is still millions. Other frontier runs (many are estimates; power at ~1.5 kW/GPU):

Opposition To Datacenters

  • Residents’ issues
    • Turbine noise
    • Water use
    • High electricity bills
  • Once built, few permanent jobs relative to the land and power footprint
  • Fear: homes subsidize grid upgrades built for AI load
  • Gallup (2026): 7 in 10 Americans oppose an AI datacenter nearby (CNBC / Gallup)
  • Memphis / Colossus — neighbors living with those effects
    • NAACP sued over gas turbines under the Clean Air Act (CNBC)

Local Action

  • Connecticut — New Haven + towns pause or limit builds (NH Independent) · (CT Insider)
  • New Jersey — 37 towns ban datacenters (NJ.com)
  • Texas — San Marcos citywide ban (KUT)
  • Missouri — Peculiar zoning block; St. Charles pause (KCUR)
  • Oregon / Washington — Hillsboro and Seattle pauses (OPB) · (Seattle)

State Action

  • New York — Gov. Hochul: ~1-year pause on new hyperscale (≥50 MW) (NY.gov)
    • First statewide hyperscale pause in the U.S.; study grid, water, bills
  • New Jersey — “Fair Share” law: datacenters pay their own power / grid costs (NJ.gov)
  • Virginia — law lets regulators shift more energy costs onto datacenters (SB 253) (LegiScan)

Federal Action

  • AI Data Center Moratorium Act — Sen. Sanders + Rep. Ocasio-Cortez: pause new or expanded AI datacenters until Congress writes national rules (S.4214) · introduced; long shot (AP)
  • Ratepayer Protection Act — Reps. Gabe Evans + Kathy Castor: big datacenters (100+ MW) cover the cost of power upgrades built for them; House passed 417–3, Senate blocked (H.R.9340)
  • GRID Savings Act — Sen. Martin Heinrich: federal energy regulators set rules so huge power users pay to plug into the grid (S.5199)
  • Power for the People Act — Sen. Chris Van Hollen: waiting line for datacenter power hookups, plus a separate rate so their costs stay on their bill (S.3682)

New Datacenters

  • Meta Hyperion (Louisiana) — up to 5 GW; >$50B; building now, full size ~2030–32 (CNBC)
  • OpenAI (Pike County, Ohio) — about 8 GW of compute capacity leased; first 800 MW targeted for 2028 (OpenAI)
  • Microsoft Fairwater (Wisconsin) — analysts project ~2+ GW of IT power by ~2028 as the campus grows (Epoch AI)
  • OpenAI Stargate (Abilene, TX) — flagship campus about 1.2 GW; more U.S. sites in the pipeline (Epoch AI)
  • For scale: 1 GW ≈ 800,000 U.S. homes (avg ~1.2 kW) — one campus can rival a big city (EIA)

Orbital Datacenters

  • Idea — compute in orbit: near-constant sun for power; radiators dump heat to space (Starcloud)
  • Starcloud — NVIDIA-backed startup with AI GPUs already in orbit (H100 chip trained and ran a model in space).
    • Big pitch: multi-GW orbital datacenter with 4 km × 4 km or solar and cooling panels (NVIDIA)(CNBC)
  • SpaceX — building orbital datacenters (SpaceX / Yahoo)
    • AI1 — SpaceX’s first orbital-datacenter design with power usage of 120 kW
    • Starship — the rocket used by SpaceX, Starcloud, and anyone else, for big launches
  • Engineering challenges
    • Radiator mass — in space you need big metal panels to radiate heat
    • Launch frequency — you have to fly all that hardware into space
    • Latency back to Earth — lag from laser transit and routing through links + ground stations

Make Agents Cheap

  • Measure — API responses report token counts; log them
  • Pick cheaper models — luna for routing; smarter only when needed
  • Caps — tools, retries, and total tokens
  • Cached tokens — reuse long prompts (~10× cheaper cached input) (OpenAI)

Cached Token Cost

  • Cached tokens let you reuse tokens in input prompt
  • Cache long instructions or text you’ll reuse (playbooks, books, tool lists)
  • Cache write - the first time you write to the tokens to the cache
    • Cache write is bit more expensive than normal input tokens
    • Once written to cache, cached input tokens are MUCH cheaper than normal input tokens

Cached Tokens

  • Put the long, unchanged stuff first in your prompt.
  • Use prompt_cache_breakpoint to a mark where your cached tokens stop
  • Everything after is normal input tokens
lease agent — cache the playbook 
PLAYBOOK = open("lease_playbook.txt").read()  # long, same every call

def ask(question: str):
    r = client.responses.create(
        model="gpt-6-luna",
        input=[
            {
                "role": "developer",
                "content": [{
                    "type": "input_text",
                    "text": PLAYBOOK,  # stable → cache here
                    "prompt_cache_breakpoint": {"mode": "explicit"},
                }],
            },
            {"role": "user", "content": question},  # changes each call
        ],
        prompt_cache_options={"mode": "explicit", "ttl": "30m"},
    )
    d = r.usage.input_tokens_details
    print("cached:", d.cached_tokens, "wrote:", d.cache_write_tokens)
    return r.output_text

ask("Can the tenant sublet?")   # 1st call: writes cache (1.25×)
ask("Who pays for HVAC?")       # next calls: mostly cached (0.1×)

Measuring Token Usage

  • OpenAI Responses API lets you measure token usage with its usage field (docs)
  • input_tokens — total tokens you sent in (prompt, tools, etc.)
  • output_tokens — total tokens the model generated (answer + hidden reasoning, if any)
  • total_tokens — input_tokens + output_tokens
  • input_tokens_details.cached_tokens — input read from the prompt cache (cheap reuse)
  • input_tokens_details.cache_write_tokens — input newly written into the cache this call
  • output_tokens_details.reasoning_tokens — internal “thinking” tokens on reasoning models (count toward output; often not shown as text)

├─ input_tokens
├─ input_tokens_details
│  ├─ cached_tokens
│  └─ cache_write_tokens
├─ output_tokens
├─ output_tokens_details
│  └─ reasoning_tokens
└─ total_tokens

Cheaper Choices

  • Newer choice models take a rich input (long text, ticket, essay) but output only a pick + probabilities — no long essay reply to pay for
  • Use this when the agent only needs to make a decision (i.e. route job to another agent)
  • Choice AI models
    • Jev (TypeSafe) — typed choice / score / yes-no with calibrated probs (Jev)
    • Decisions API (OpenAI, preview) — Luna-powered picks among predefined answers (overview)
